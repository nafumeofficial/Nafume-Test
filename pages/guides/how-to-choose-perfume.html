Buying Guide

How to Choose the Right Perfume for Yourself

By NAFUME  ·  Fragrance Guide  ·  June 2026

Choosing a perfume without being able to smell it first is a real challenge — and most people in India are buying online, which means reading descriptions and trusting reviews rather than actually trying before buying. The good news is that you can significantly narrow down your choices if you understand a few basic things about how fragrance is categorised and what actually determines whether a scent will suit you.

This guide covers the fundamentals — fragrance families, occasion-matching, skin chemistry, and how to make a confident choice even without testing in person.

Start With Fragrance Families

Fragrances are broadly grouped into families based on their dominant character. These aren't strict rules, but they're a useful shorthand for figuring out what you're drawn to:

  • Fresh / Aquatic — Clean, light, often marine or citrus-forward. Easy to wear, broadly likeable, works well in warm weather. Example: Aqua Manthan.
  • Woody / Aromatic — Cedarwood, sandalwood, vetiver, herbs. Warm but not sweet, works across genders and occasions. Often the most wearable category day-to-day.
  • Spicy / Oriental — Cinnamon, pepper, cardamom, amber, warm resins. Bold, confident, better suited to cooler months and evenings. Example: Red Spirit.
  • Floral — Rose, jasmine, iris, lily, musky florals. Ranges from light and fresh to heavy and complex. Seduction sits in warm floral-oriental territory.
  • Gourmand — Coffee, vanilla, caramel, chocolate. Distinctive and wearable as personal fragrance, but can be polarising. Example: All Day's coffee-tobacco dry-down.
  • Oud / Woody Intense — Deep, rich, smoky, long-lasting. High presence, best for evenings and cooler conditions. Example: Dark Oud.

Match the Fragrance to When You'll Wear It

The same fragrance wears differently depending on environment. An important question to answer honestly: is this for daily use, a specific occasion, or both? A fragrance you wear every day needs to be something you won't tire of quickly. A fragrance for special occasions can afford to be bolder and more memorable.

Think About What You're Already Drawn To

If you already have a favourite fragrance — even if it's something you've worn for years — pay attention to what it is about it that you like. Is it the freshness of the opening, the warmth of the dry-down, or a specific note like cedar or vanilla? This tells you more about your fragrance preferences than any quiz will.

Similarly, think about smells you're naturally drawn to outside of perfume — certain foods, environments, materials. People who love the smell of rain often love aquatic fragrances. People drawn to wood and leather often gravitate toward woody or oud-inspired scents.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I choose between different perfumes?

Start by identifying what you want the fragrance for — daily wear, office, evenings, or special occasions. Then think about what kinds of smells you're naturally drawn to: fresh and clean, warm and woody, sweet, spicy, or floral. Use these as filters rather than trying to evaluate everything at once.

Does skin type affect how a perfume smells?

Yes. Oily skin tends to hold fragrance longer and can amplify certain notes. Dry skin may need slightly more fragrance for the same longevity. Applying an unscented moisturiser before perfume helps dry skin hold the fragrance better. Body heat and pH also subtly affect how a fragrance develops.

Is there a perfume that works for both men and women?

Yes — unisex fragrances are built to work across body chemistries and personal styles. From the NAFUME range, Aqua Manthan and All Day both work naturally across genders. Fresh, woody, and clean-musk fragrances tend to be the most versatile in this regard.

How many perfumes do I actually need?

Most people find two or three covers most situations — a lighter, fresher scent for daytime and warm weather, and something deeper for evenings and cooler months. A single versatile fragrance works fine as a starting point.
